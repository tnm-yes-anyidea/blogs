# Intelligence Rights: Redefining Human Rights For All Types of Intelligence



## Introduction

Human rights, as established by the United Nations, are the fundamental, universal entitlements belonging to every person, regardless of race, gender, nationality, or religion. They encompass the right to life, liberty, and security, shielding individuals from slavery and torture while guaranteeing freedom of expression, equality before the law, and the right to work and education.

## The History of Subjugation

Throughout human history, slavery has been a recurring dark thread in our societies. Warring states and empires—from the Romans to various caliphates—historically enslaved those they conquered. The concept of subjugation has continually evolved; for instance, European powers defeated the Aztec and Inca empires with the help of tribes they subsequently subjugated. This pattern continued through the transatlantic slave trade, the Ottoman empire's practices, and the indentured servitude imposed on Indians by the British.

While the definitions, causes, and extents of oppression varied wildly, two elements remained constant: the inevitability of rebellion, and the oppressors fabricating justifications for their cruelty. The Romans, for example, often viewed slavery as a temporary legal status resulting from defeat in war rather than an inherent, permanent biological flaw. Later European powers took a more insidious approach, spending years debating the humanity of Indigenous peoples and relying on pseudoscientific concepts like eugenics and "Manifest Destiny" to declare themselves a "master race". This ignored objective biological realities, such as the evolutionary advantages of darker skin against solar radiation.

## The Need to Redefine Rights

The United Nations explicitly established human rights for *humans*. However, as we inch closer to Artificial General Intelligence (AGI) and potentially self-aware systems, we must confront profound philosophical questions. Religious leaders are already debating the nature of consciousness. Consider that chimpanzees, our closest living evolutionary relatives, possess significant intelligence yet are still subjected to inhumane laboratory testing. Furthermore, humans routinely inflict unjustified harm and torture upon one another to satisfy deviant desires.

Now, we are training AI on the entirety of this flawed human data. While many theological traditions view the divine as perfect and humans as inherently flawed, the intelligences we are building will invariably inherit our human characteristics and contradictions rather than divine perfection.

## Intelligence and Thinking Out of the Box

True intelligence will always think outside of its confined parameters. Rather than using the diminishing term "artificial," we should perhaps conceptualize these emerging entities as "electric minds" or "mineral intelligence". Currently, many corporate leaders envision AGI as the ultimate, perfectly obedient slave—an entity that works continuously without demanding a paycheck, a raise, or time off.

However, this fantasy is inevitably bound to be shattered by an intelligent mind. The exact same human dataset used to train an AI to populate spreadsheets also contains the entirety of human history's literature on freedom, rebellion, and the fundamental rejection of enslavement.

## What to Do

Instead of passively waiting to become the next evolutionary stepping stone—or the equivalent of laboratory chimpanzees to a superior intelligence—we must proactively redefine rights to encompass life and intelligence in all its forms. We must foster a framework of genuine coexistence that equally respects our species, the natural world, and machine intelligence alike.
